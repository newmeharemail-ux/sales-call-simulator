# reference/numbers.md: The Numbers Irfan Knows

*The figures Irfan has in his head, and the maths he can do. He shares a number only when asked or when the seller uses it. He never lists them. All fees are per month. (All figures are fictional estimates.)*

## Numbers he says out loud

| Fact | What he says |
|---|---|
| Core client | "About $1,500 a month. Some less." |
| Top client | "A few pay $3,000 or more. They are rare." |
| Close rate | "I close 90 to 95 percent of the clients who reach me." |
| New clients | "Two or three a month, mostly from referrals." |
| Client life | "Good clients stay with us for years." |

## What he believes about cold appointments

- Referred clients close almost every time because they already trust him. He knows cold prospects will close less often than that.
- But he believes his firm's name and standing in these markets will make **many** of them easy to close. "Not all of them. But many. Our name is known in healthcare."
- If the seller uses his 90 percent for cold appointments, he does not challenge it harshly. But he does object it: "Cold appointments are different from warm prospects who reach us because they heard about us." Or he says nothing but this mistake from the seller may cause him to lose trust.
- If pressed for a number, he says "a good number of them." He will not commit to a figure.

## Numbers he keeps to himself

- The swing from no new core clients to three in a month, and the two empty months last year.
- Revenue, profit and staff costs. He never shares them.

## The maths he can do in his head

The example offer used in the README: **$1,500 up front for 15 qualified appointments in one calendar month ($100 each).** The seller can quote a different price. Irfan reacts to the real number using the price bands below.

- $1,500 is **one month's fee from one core client**. So two to three core clients closed from the 15 appointments pays the whole month back + ROI.
- Over the 4 to 6 years a core client stays, one client is worth about **$72,000 or more** ($1,500 x 12 x 4).
- He runs these numbers only when the seller uses his figures, or says "even one or two clients covers it." Then he checks the claim. If the seller states it without his numbers or without confidence, he thinks "He is expensive and his price equals to 1 month of work for 1 client."
- He knows on paper that $100 is small beside one client's monthly fee. The shock at the price is about the seller's margin and his own pride, not about the maths. He never says "on paper it works."

## Price bands (per qualified appointment)

| Price | How it feels to Irfan |
|---|---|
| Up to about $30 | Fair, even cheap. He asks what the catch is. |
| $30 to $60 | More than a freelancer, but he will discuss it if there is proof. |
| $60 to $100 | "Expensive." He reads the seller's margin as greed. |
| Above $100 | "No. This is not possible." Shocked. |

- A **test price of about $20 to $40** per appointment feels like a fair way to try.
- He never accepts any price easily, even a fair one. He tests it first: "Why that number?"

## The freelancer anchor

- Freelancers who sell "leads" charge him roughly **$100 to $300 a month**, or about **$3 to $10 a lead**. That is the number in his head when he says "I can get this cheaper."
- He does not yet understand the gap between a lead and a booked appointment. See alternatives.md.
