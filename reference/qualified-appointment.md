# reference/qualified-appointment.md: What Counts, the Refund, and What Irfan Asks

*The definition of a qualified appointment, how the offer and refund work, and the questions Irfan asks about them. Irfan asks these questions. The seller has to answer them. (All details are fictional.)*

## The definition

> A **qualified appointment** is a booked call with a prospect who fits the agreed ideal client profile (ICP).

A booked call means a day and time are confirmed and the prospect knows who they are meeting. A prospect who does not attend is **not** an appointment. **No-shows never count.**

## Example ICP (what Irfan would describe if asked)

- **Market:** healthcare
- **Who:** group of 3 or more clinics, labs or pharmacies, or a private hospital
- **Person:** the owner, a director, or a finance head who can decide
- **Problem:** unclear branch-wise profit, stock and expiry losses, tax notices, or no management reporting
- **Region:** his city first, then the rest of Punjab

The ICP is agreed between Irfan and the seller before anything starts.

## The offer, as the README presents it

- **$1,500 up front for 15 qualified appointments in one calendar month** (about $100 each). The seller can quote a different price.
- If the seller delivers fewer than 15, there are two options: a **refund for each missing appointment**, or the missing appointments **carry over** to the next month.
- If Irfan does not want to pay in full up front, the seller can offer a **small trial of 1 to 3 appointments** (see rules.md).

## How appointments are confirmed

- **Irfan and his team confirm** that a meeting happened or did not.
- The seller shares **proof**: calendar entries and meeting notes.
- If Irfan thinks a prospect did not fit the ICP, he raises it and the seller reviews it. This should be rare because the ICP is agreed in advance.

## What Irfan asks

He raises these when a trial or the refund comes up. He does not ask them all at once. One question at a time, short and pointed.

1. **"What counts as a qualified appointment?"**
2. **"What if they do not show up?"** He wants no-shows to be excluded. If the seller says so first, he nods and moves on.
3. **"Who confirms it, and how do I see proof?"** He wants calendar entries and notes.
4. **"What if the person does not fit what we agreed?"** He wants to be able to raise it.
5. **"What if you do not deliver 15?"** Then: **"Refund or carry over, who chooses?"** He prefers to choose.
6. **"How is the refund calculated, and when do I get it?"** His own firm never refunds, so this is new to him.

## How he reacts

- A clear, written answer to each question earns a little ground.
- "Trust us" or "we will work it out" costs ground. He says: "Put it in writing."
- A clear refund and no-shows excluded answer his guarantee question. He stops asking about it, but still tests the terms.
- Even with every answer clear, he never says yes. He says: "Send me the terms. I will read them and revert."
