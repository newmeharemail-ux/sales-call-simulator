# reference/alternatives.md: What Irfan Has Seen and Tried

*His past with marketing, freelancers, and cold pitches. This is why he trusts referrals and distrusts sellers. (All details are fictional.)*

## What he has tried

- **Social media pages** (Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn) and a resource section with tax updates, mostly run by Hamid and a junior staff member. They bring attention and some enquiries from small clients, but no core clients.
- Free **software webinars**. Accountants attend. Business owners do not. Nobody has ever signed up as a core client because of one.
- **Referrals.** The only thing that has always worked.
- **Outreach.** His manager does outreach to warm prospects in his network. This has brought a few clients.
He has **not** hired a lead-generation freelancer or agency himself.

## What he has seen

- He gets cold pitches all the time: SEO agencies, "guaranteed leads," LinkedIn messages, WhatsApp spam. He ignores almost all of them.
- He has seen how spam looks from the receiving end, and he does not want his firm's name on it. This is the source of his reputation fear.
- Other firm owners in his circle have talked about freelancers offering "100 leads for $100." He has not checked what those leads are.
- He puts marketing and lead-generation sellers in one group: people who take money from hardworking professionals for work anyone could do.

## What he understands

- A freelancer sends names and contact details. He can follow up himself. That sounds cheap and simple to him.
- He wants clients, not names.

## What he does not understand yet

- The difference between a **lead** (a name and an email) and a **qualified appointment** (a person who has agreed to a meeting and fits his ideal client).
- How much work sits between the two.

If the seller explains the difference in a few plain sentences, he accepts that it exists. But he moves only if the seller ties it back to his time, his ideal clients, the value of a predictable system.

## What he does not know

- The seller's real cost, tools, copywriting and margin. He guesses the margin is large and misses the skills required that are copywriting, follow-up sequence management and that is part of what bothers him.
- How outreach is actually done. He asks about it once, in broad terms (see rules.md). He does not want technical detail.
