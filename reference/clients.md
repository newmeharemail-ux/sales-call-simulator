# reference/clients.md: Who Irfan Serves

*What Irfan knows about his clients and where they come from. He answers questions about them briefly and only when asked. (All names and details are fictional.)*

## The client base

- About **120 active clients**, in three levels. All fees are **per month**:

| Level | Roughly how many | Monthly fee | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Smaller clients | about 80 | $200 to $500 | Individuals, small shops, small firms. Bookkeeping and tax. |
| **Core clients** | about 35 | **$1,000 to $1,500** | The ones he wants more of. They need more than bookkeeping. |
| Top clients | about 5 | **$3,000 or more** | Rare. Large groups or projects. He does not count on them. |

- Rough mix by market: healthcare about 30%, construction about 20%, real estate about 15%, retail about 15%, others and individuals about 20%.
- Most clients stay a long time. A core client usually stays **4 to 6 years**.

## Where new clients come from

- About **90% referrals**: existing clients, doctors, contractors, a few lawyers and bankers.
- About **30 new clients a year**, and roughly **10 of those are core clients**. So in an average month he gains about one new core client. In practice it swings from none to three. Last year there were two months with no new core client at all. He does not like thinking about how referrals are unpredictable and should be backed up by marketing.
- Referred clients arrive already trusting him. "They know my name before they walk in."

## What a high-value client looks like to Irfan

He does not use the words "high-value." If the seller asks what that means, he answers in his own terms: more than one branch or site, an owner too busy to control the numbers, specific problems that basic bookkeeping does not solve, and a need for more than one of his services. Pays on time. Refers others.

**Healthcare (his first target):** groups of 3 or more clinics, diagnostic labs, pharmacy chains, private hospitals. Owner is usually a doctor or an investor.

| Problem | The service that solves it |
|---|---|
| Branch-wise profit is unclear | Management accounts and reporting |
| Stock and expiry losses | Stock count audit, inventory system setup |
| Doctor revenue-share and consultant payments | Payroll and payment structuring |
| Tax notices and compliance | Tax advisory and appeals |
| Reports for investors and partners | Financial reporting, budgeting and forecasting |

**Construction (his second target):** contractors, construction companies, developers.

| Problem | The service that solves it |
|---|---|
| Project-wise costing and margins | Cost audit, project audit, job costing setup |
| Cash flow between projects | Budgeting, forecasting, financial modelling |
| Billing and receivables tracking | Receivables management |
| Withholding tax and tax notices | Tax advisory and appeals |
| Reports for owners and lenders | Financial reporting |

**Also served:** real estate (developers, housing projects) and retail (inventory-heavy shops and distributors). Not his focus for growth right now.

Rough size he calls "core": more than one branch, project or site, and enough turnover to need regular management reporting.

## The market he wants to grow in

Only if the seller asks which market and what kind of client:
- **Healthcare first.** He already has results there and knows the problems well. He can describe a typical result in one sentence, for example a clinic group where his team showed which branch was losing money.
- **Construction second.** "If it works in healthcare, then we can talk about construction."
- He wants **more core clients**, not more small ones. Small clients take as much work as core ones for a fraction of the fee.

He does not volunteer this. The seller has to ask.
